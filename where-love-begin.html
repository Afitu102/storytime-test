Chapter 1 — The Stranger at the Gate

Clara had always believed that the safest place for her heart was inside a locked room.

She had learned that lesson the hard way.

At twenty-seven, Clara had everything people could see.

She had the beauty that made strangers turn their heads twice. She had the kind of smile that could soften an angry room, even though she rarely gave it away freely. She lived in a beautiful house in Lagos, drove a car that most people only admired from a distance, dressed elegantly, and worked hard to maintain the life she had built for herself.

From the outside, her life looked almost perfect.

But Clara knew better.

There was a difference between having everything and having someone.

She had everything.

She had never had someone.

At least, not someone who stayed.

She stood by the large glass window of her bedroom that evening, staring at the lights scattered across the Lagos skyline.

The city was alive.

Cars moved along the roads below. Music floated faintly from somewhere in the neighborhood. A generator hummed in the distance. People were laughing, arguing, rushing home, meeting friends, chasing money, chasing dreams.

Clara watched all of it without really seeing any of it.

Her phone vibrated on the dressing table.

She looked at it.

Another message.

She already knew who it was.

She picked up the phone and read the message.

"Clara, please give me another chance. I swear I'm different."

She stared at the words for several seconds.

Then she laughed quietly.

Not because it was funny.

Because it wasn't.

It was painfully familiar.

She had heard those words before.

From men who said they loved her.

From men who called her their queen.

From men who promised that money meant nothing to them.

And somehow, after a few months, money became the only thing that mattered.

Clara placed the phone back on the table.

"Different," she whispered.

She had heard that word too many times.

Her first serious relationship had ended when she discovered that the man she loved had been secretly using her bank card.

The second had asked her to finance a business that disappeared almost immediately after she transferred the money.

The third had proposed marriage.

That one hurt the most.

She had believed him.

She had allowed herself to imagine a future.

Then, three weeks before their wedding introduction, she discovered that he had another woman.

Clara had walked away without begging.

But something inside her had never completely recovered.

Since then, whenever a man approached her, she automatically asked herself the same question.

What does he want?

Love?

Her money?

Her connections?

Her lifestyle?

Or perhaps all of them?

She had stopped believing in genuine answers.

"Clara!"

Her mother's voice came from downstairs.

"Coming!"

Clara took one last look at her phone before leaving the bedroom.

She didn't know it yet, but that evening would be the last ordinary evening of her life for a very long time.

The next morning began like every other morning.

Clara woke up early.

She showered, dressed for work, drank half a cup of coffee, and left the house before eight.

Her driver was unavailable that morning, so she decided to drive herself.

She was already late.

Her meeting at the office was scheduled for nine.

At eight-thirty-seven, while she was driving through a busy part of Lagos, her car suddenly made a strange noise.

Clara frowned.

"Not today."

She slowed down.

The car moved another few meters.

Then it stopped.

Completely.

Clara tried starting it again.

Nothing.

She tried again.

Still nothing.

"You have got to be kidding me."

She grabbed her phone and called the mechanic.

No answer.

She called again.

Nothing.

Clara sighed and stepped out of the car.

The morning heat was already beginning to rise.

She stood beside her car in her office clothes, looking completely out of place among the busy roadside.

She opened the bonnet.

Then she stared at the engine.

She had absolutely no idea what she was looking at.

"Wonderful."

She closed the bonnet.

A few people walked past.

Some looked at the expensive car.

Others looked at Clara.

One man even stopped and asked if she needed help.

Clara politely refused.

She had learned not to trust random men who suddenly appeared whenever she was vulnerable.

She called her office.

"I'm going to be late," she told her secretary.

"Again?"

Clara closed her eyes.

"Please don't start."

Her secretary laughed.

"Madam, I'm not starting anything. Just come safely."

Clara ended the call.

She was about to call a taxi when she heard a voice behind her.

"Madam."

She turned.

A young man stood a few steps away.

He wore a simple shirt and dark trousers. His appearance was neat but ordinary. He didn't look like the men Clara usually met at expensive restaurants, corporate events, or social gatherings.

Beside him was a green and yellow keke.

Clara immediately understood.

A keke driver.

"Yes?"

"Your car has a problem?"

Clara glanced at the car.

"Yes."

He nodded.

"Can I take a look?"

Clara hesitated.

Her instincts told her to say no.

But something about the young man's voice was different.

There was no flirting.

No unnecessary smile.

No attempt to impress her.

Just a simple question.

"You know about cars?"

The young man smiled.

"Small small."

Clara almost smiled.

"Small small?"

"Yes. If I say I know everything, you'll think I'm a mechanic."

For the first time that morning, Clara laughed.

"Okay. Go ahead."

He walked toward the car.

"My name is Ken, by the way."

"Clara."

He nodded.

"Nice to meet you, Clara."

He opened the bonnet and examined the engine.

Clara stood beside him, watching.

After a few seconds, Ken asked her to try starting the car.

She entered the driver's seat and turned the key.

The engine came alive.

Clara stared.

"What did you do?"

Ken shrugged.

"Nothing serious. The connection was loose."

Clara looked impressed.

"How much do I owe you?"

Ken shook his head.

"Nothing."

Clara frowned.

"Nothing?"

"You can keep your money."

Something about that answer caught her attention.

Men didn't usually refuse Clara's money.

Especially men who knew how expensive her car was.

"At least let me give you something."

Ken smiled.

"If you really want to thank me, just be careful on the road."

He closed the bonnet.

Clara watched him return toward his keke.

"Wait."

Ken turned.

Clara didn't know why she stopped him.

"Can I have your number?"

Ken looked slightly surprised.

"My number?"

"Yes. In case the car develops another problem."

Ken smiled.

"So I'm your emergency mechanic now?"

Clara rolled her eyes.

"Just give me the number."

He gave it to her.

Clara saved it as Ken — Keke Driver.

She didn't think much about it.

At least, she thought she didn't.

That evening, after work, Clara found herself looking at his contact.

She didn't call.

She simply stared at the name for a few seconds.

Then she shook her head.

"What am I doing?"

She put the phone away.

Two days passed.

Ken didn't call.

Three days passed.

Still nothing.

Clara found that strange.

Every man who got her number usually found an excuse to call.

Some sent good morning messages.

Some asked where she was.

Some immediately started talking about how beautiful she was.

Ken did none of that.

It bothered her.

More than she wanted to admit.

One Friday evening, Clara was sitting alone at a restaurant when she noticed him.

Ken was standing outside.

He wasn't looking for her.

He was simply waiting for passengers.

Clara watched him from behind the glass.

Something made her step outside.

"Ken."

He turned.

His face brightened with recognition.

"Clara."

"You remembered my name."

"Why wouldn't I?"

She smiled.

"You never called."

Ken looked confused.

"You didn't ask me to."

Clara didn't know what to say.

That answer was so simple.

Yet it felt strangely powerful.

Most men saw her number as an invitation.

Ken apparently saw it as just a number.

"Are you always this serious?" Clara asked.

"Are you always this curious?"

She laughed.

"Maybe."

They stood there talking for several minutes.

Nothing extraordinary happened.

No dramatic music.

No sudden declaration of love.

Just two people talking.

And somehow, that simple conversation stayed in Clara's mind long after she returned home.

That night, she lay in bed staring at the ceiling.

She remembered Ken's laugh.

His calmness.

The way he spoke without trying to impress her.

She smiled without realizing it.

Then she caught herself.

"No."

She turned to the other side.

"Absolutely not."

She wasn't going to make the same mistake again.

She wasn't going to fall for another man.

Especially not a man she barely knew.

Especially not a keke driver.

But Clara didn't know something.

Ken wasn't who she thought he was.

The next morning, Clara was sitting at breakfast when her mother entered the room with an envelope in her hand.

"Clara."

She looked up.

"Yes, Mum?"

Her mother placed the envelope on the table.

"Someone left this for you."

Clara frowned.

"Who?"

"I don't know."

Clara picked up the envelope.

There was no name on it.

No address.

Nothing.

She slowly opened it.

Inside was a single photograph.

Clara's heart stopped.

It was a photograph of Ken.

But he wasn't wearing his ordinary clothes.

He was standing in front of a large university building.

And beside him was a woman Clara had never seen before.

On the back of the photograph, someone had written four words.

"You don't know him."

Clara's fingers began to tremble.

She looked at the photograph again.

For the first time since meeting Ken, she felt something other than curiosity.

She felt fear.

Who had sent the photograph?

Who was the woman standing beside Ken?

And most importantly...

What exactly had Ken been hiding from her?
