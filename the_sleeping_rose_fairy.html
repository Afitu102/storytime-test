🌹 The Sleeping Rose Fairy

🧚 Fairy Tale • 🕒 15 min read

🌹 Chapter One – The Rose That Never Closed

Long ago, beyond the tallest hills and the oldest forests, there was a secret garden that no ordinary person had ever seen. It was called the Garden of Endless Flowers. Every flower there bloomed every day of the year. None of them ever dried, and none ever lost their sweet smell.

The birds sang from sunrise until the stars filled the sky. Butterflies of every colour danced from flower to flower, and the wind always carried the soft smell of roses, lilies and jasmine.

In the very centre of the garden stood one beautiful red rose.

It was not the biggest flower.

It was not the brightest flower.

But everyone who entered the garden noticed it first.

Its petals looked as soft as silk, and every morning tiny drops of dew rested on them like little diamonds.

There was one more secret about that rose.

Inside it slept a tiny fairy.

Her name was Luma.

She was no bigger than a little bird. Her hair shone like silver in the moonlight, and her wings sparkled whenever sunlight touched them.

But Luma had been asleep for a very, very long time.

No one remembered when she closed her eyes.

Even the oldest trees could not tell the story.

Some flowers believed she had slept for one hundred years.

Others believed it had been much longer.

Every morning the birds landed beside the rose.

They sang happy songs, hoping she would wake.

Every evening the butterflies rested on the petals, hoping she would open her eyes.

But nothing ever happened.

She simply slept peacefully inside her beautiful flower.

The animals loved watching over her.

The rabbits quietly hopped around the rose so they would never damage it.

The squirrels never climbed the branches above it because they feared dropping nuts onto the flower.

Even the bees flew gently whenever they came close.

The whole garden protected the sleeping fairy.

No one knew why.

They only knew that she was important.

One bright morning, something unusual happened.

A little girl named Elia wandered into the forest while picking wild berries with her grandmother.

She loved flowers more than anything in the world.

Whenever she found a beautiful flower, she stopped to admire it instead of picking it.

As she walked deeper into the woods, she noticed something shining between the trees.

It looked like tiny stars floating in the air.

Curious, Elia slowly followed the glowing lights.

The farther she walked, the brighter they became.

Soon she reached a small opening hidden behind thick green vines.

She gently pushed the vines aside.

Her eyes became wide.

She had never seen anything so beautiful.

Flowers covered every corner.

Blue flowers.

Golden flowers.

Purple flowers.

Flowers that softly glowed without any sunlight.

Tiny butterflies with sparkling wings floated through the air.

Crystal-clear water flowed through little streams that sang as they moved over smooth stones.

Elia slowly whispered,

"This place is magical..."

She carefully walked deeper into the garden.

Every flower seemed to turn towards her.

The birds became quiet.

Even the wind slowed down.

Almost as though the garden itself was watching her.

Then...

She saw it.

The beautiful red rose standing alone in the middle of the garden.

Its petals glowed softly in the sunlight.

Elia slowly walked closer.

She smiled.

"It is the most beautiful flower I've ever seen."

Just as she leaned forward to admire it...

She noticed something inside the rose.

A tiny girl...

Curled up peacefully.

Sleeping.

With shining wings folded behind her.

Elia covered her mouth in surprise.

"A fairy..."

Without warning...

One glowing petal slowly fell from the rose.

It landed gently in Elia's hand.

The moment she touched it...

A soft whisper floated through the garden.

"Please... don't let me sleep forever..."

🌙 End of Chapter One

✨ Chapter Two – The Tiny Sleeping Fairy

Elia looked around quickly.

The whisper had sounded so real that she almost believed someone was standing behind her.

But the garden was quiet again.

The birds had stopped singing.

The butterflies floated gently through the air as though nothing had happened.

Only the glowing petal resting in her hand continued to shine.

She carefully looked back at the tiny fairy sleeping inside the rose.

Luma had not moved.

She still rested peacefully with both hands beneath her tiny face.

Her silver hair covered part of her cheek.

She looked so calm that Elia wondered if she had imagined the whisper.

Slowly, she placed the glowing petal back onto the rose.

The petal floated gently into place by itself.

For a second, the entire flower glowed brighter.

Then everything returned to normal.

Elia smiled.

"You really are magical."

Just then, she heard a tiny cough.

She turned around quickly.

Standing on a nearby mushroom was an old grasshopper wearing a tiny green hat.

He leaned on a walking stick made from a thin flower stem.

His long white eyebrows almost covered his eyes.

He looked at Elia for several seconds before speaking.

"So..."

"You are the child the garden has been waiting for."

Elia blinked.

"Me?"

The old grasshopper nodded slowly.

"I never thought you would arrive so soon."

Elia carefully walked closer.

"Do you know the fairy?"

The old grasshopper sighed.

"I have watched over her longer than I can remember."

"My name is Elder Hopper."

"I have guarded this garden ever since Luma fell asleep."

Elia looked at the sleeping fairy once again.

"Why won't she wake up?"

The old grasshopper became quiet.

His eyes slowly looked toward the sky.

"Many years ago..."

"A dark shadow entered this garden."

"It was jealous."

"It hated beauty."

"It hated kindness."

"It hated happiness."

"So it placed a sleeping spell upon the fairy who protected this garden."

Elia listened carefully.

"Nobody could break the spell?"

The old grasshopper slowly shook his head.

"Many have tried."

"The birds sang."

"The flowers bloomed."

"The stars even sent their light."

"But nothing worked."

Elia looked at Luma again.

"There must be something."

"There is always a way."

The old grasshopper smiled for the first time.

"That is exactly what she would have said."

He slowly reached into a small bag hanging beside him.

From inside it, he pulled out an old golden key.

It was tiny.

Almost small enough for the fairy herself to carry.

The key sparkled with little stars carved into it.

Elder Hopper placed it gently into Elia's hand.

"This key opens the Moon Blossom Gate."

"Behind that gate grows the only flower that can break the sleeping spell."

Elia's eyes lit up.

"So I just need to find it?"

The old grasshopper slowly nodded.

"If only it were that simple."

"The flower blooms only once every hundred years."

"And tonight..."

"...is the only night it will bloom again."

Before Elia could ask another question, a cold wind suddenly swept across the garden.

The flowers bent low.

The butterflies quickly disappeared.

Dark clouds slowly covered the bright afternoon sky.

The old grasshopper looked frightened.

"No..."

"It has found you."

Elia frowned.

"Who?"

Before he could answer...

A loud laugh echoed through the entire garden.

"Hahahahaha..."

It was deep.

Cold.

And it seemed to come from every direction at once.

The roses slowly closed their petals.

The colourful flowers lost their glow.

Even the beautiful garden suddenly looked afraid.

High above the trees...

A huge shadow spread across the sky.

Watching them.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🧚 Chapter Three – The Fairy's Secret

Elia could not move.

The dark shadow covered almost the whole garden. It moved slowly across the sky like a giant black cloud, yet there was no sound except the cold wind blowing through the flowers.

The birds flew into the trees and hid.

The rabbits disappeared beneath the bushes.

Even the butterflies stopped flying.

The whole garden became silent.

Only the old grasshopper stood beside Elia.

His tiny hands trembled as he held his walking stick.

"It has returned..."

Elia swallowed nervously.

"Who is it?"

The old grasshopper looked up at the sky.

"The Shadow Crow."

"It was once the brightest bird in all the fairy world."

"It loved being admired."

"But when everyone began praising Fairy Luma for making flowers bloom and bringing joy to every living thing..."

"...jealousy filled its heart."

"It wanted all the beauty for itself."

"So it searched for dark magic."

"And that dark magic changed it forever."

As Elder Hopper finished speaking, the shadow slowly landed on the highest tree in the garden.

Its feathers looked like smoke.

Its eyes glowed dark purple.

Its wings were larger than any eagle Elia had ever seen.

When it folded them, darkness spread over the flowers below.

The Shadow Crow laughed again.

"So..."

"Someone finally found the sleeping fairy."

Its voice sounded smooth, but there was something frightening hidden inside it.

Elia stood in front of the rose without thinking.

The crow noticed.

It smiled.

"You are brave."

"But bravery does not always save people."

The old grasshopper stepped forward.

"You have already done enough."

"You cursed this garden."

"You have no place here anymore."

The Shadow Crow laughed louder.

"Cursed?"

"I simply gave everyone what they deserved."

"Beauty should belong to only one."

"If flowers bloom everywhere..."

"...then none of them are special."

Elia shook her head.

"That's not true."

The crow looked at her.

"Oh?"

Elia took a deep breath.

"My grandmother grows many flowers."

"They are all different."

"That doesn't make them less beautiful."

"It makes the garden more beautiful."

For a moment, the crow became quiet.

Then it smiled again.

"You speak like Luma."

"I hated that."

Without warning, the Shadow Crow spread its enormous wings.

A strong wind rushed through the garden.

Several flowers lost their petals.

The glowing streams became dark.

The old trees bent under the powerful wind.

Elia almost lost her balance.

She quickly held the rose so it would not fall.

As soon as her hands touched the flower...

The tiny golden key inside her pocket began to glow.

The light became brighter.

Brighter.

Until the crow suddenly stopped laughing.

Its purple eyes became wide.

"The Moon Blossom Key..."

"It still exists?"

The old grasshopper smiled for the first time since the crow arrived.

"The garden has chosen its protector."

The Shadow Crow's face became angry.

It opened its wings again.

"You must never reach the Moon Blossom."

"If Luma wakes..."

"...everything I have waited for will disappear."

The crow flew high into the dark clouds.

Within seconds, it vanished.

The cold wind slowly stopped.

Sunlight returned.

The flowers slowly lifted their heads again.

The butterflies returned one after another.

But nobody smiled.

Everyone knew the Shadow Crow had not given up.

Elder Hopper gently looked at Elia.

"We cannot wait any longer."

"The Moon Blossom will bloom before midnight."

"And the path to it..."

"...is more dangerous than anything you have ever imagined."

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🌙 Chapter Four – Into the Moonlit Garden

Elder Hopper did not waste another second.

"Come with me," he said.

He hopped quickly across the garden, much faster than Elia thought an old grasshopper could move.

She hurried after him, holding the tiny golden key tightly in her hand.

They walked past streams that sparkled like glass.

They crossed bridges made from flower vines.

The deeper they went, the quieter the garden became.

Soon, the colourful flowers disappeared.

Instead, tall white trees stood on both sides of the narrow path.

Their leaves glowed softly, making the forest look as though hundreds of little lanterns were hanging above them.

"It is beautiful," Elia whispered.

"It is," Elder Hopper replied.

"But beauty can also hide danger."

Elia looked around carefully.

Everything seemed peaceful.

Too peaceful.

Not a single bird sang.

No butterflies floated by.

Even the wind had disappeared.

After walking for a long time, they reached an old stone bridge.

The bridge stretched across a deep river that shimmered silver under the evening sky.

Unlike the peaceful streams in the garden, this river moved slowly without making any sound.

Elia leaned over the edge.

She could not even see her own reflection.

Instead...

She saw strange moving shadows beneath the water.

They looked almost like people walking below the surface.

She stepped back immediately.

"What is that?"

Elder Hopper sighed.

"The River of Forgotten Dreams."

"It steals memories from anyone who falls inside."

Elia quickly held the bridge rail.

"I definitely don't want to fall."

The old grasshopper smiled.

"Neither do I."

Together they slowly crossed the bridge.

Halfway across...

A sweet voice floated through the air.

"Elia..."

She stopped walking.

The voice sounded familiar.

Very familiar.

It sounded exactly like her mother.

"Elia... come back..."

"My dear child..."

Elia turned around.

Nobody was there.

Only the empty bridge.

Then she heard it again.

"I'm waiting for you..."

Tears almost filled her eyes.

"My mother..."

She took one step backward.

Elder Hopper quickly grabbed her dress.

"No!"

"Do not listen."

"It isn't your mother."

"It's the river trying to trick you."

Elia covered her ears.

The voice became louder.

"Please..."

"I'm lonely..."

"Come to me..."

Elia squeezed her eyes shut.

She remembered something her grandmother always told her.

"Not every voice that calls your name belongs to someone who loves you."

She took a deep breath.

Then she began walking forward again.

Step by step.

The voice slowly faded away.

Finally...

They reached the other side of the bridge.

The moment Elia's feet touched the ground, the strange voices disappeared completely.

She let out a long breath.

"I almost believed it."

Elder Hopper nodded.

"Everyone does."

"That river has fooled many travellers."

He looked toward the dark forest ahead.

"The hardest part of the journey begins now."

Elia followed his eyes.

Far ahead stood an enormous silver gate covered with glowing vines.

The moon had just begun rising behind it.

The golden key inside her hand suddenly became warm.

Very warm.

Elder Hopper smiled.

"The Moon Blossom Gate has found its keeper."

But before either of them could take another step...

A loud flap of giant wings echoed across the forest.

The Shadow Crow had returned.

And this time...

It was not alone.

🌙 End of Chapter Four
